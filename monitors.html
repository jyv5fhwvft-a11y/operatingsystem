Monitors

A high-level synchronization construct that wraps shared data inside a protective room — one thread inside at a time, with condition variables for polite waiting.

The Definition

A Monitor is a synchronization construct that combines a mutex with one or more condition variables. It encapsulates shared data together with the procedures that operate on that data, and guarantees that at most one thread executes inside the monitor at any time. Threads inside call wait(cv) to atomically release the lock and block, and signal(cv) to wake a waiter — who must then re-acquire the lock before continuing.

🎈 The 5th Grader Analogy
Imagine a bathroom with one key. Only the kid holding the key can go inside — that's the monitor. Everyone else waits in line outside.

If you get inside and discover there's no toilet paper, you can't just sit there hogging the key. You step out to the waiting bench (that's wait()) and hand the key to the next kid. When the janitor restocks (signal()), you wake up and rejoin the queue.

📐 The Technical Analogy
A monitor = implicit mutex + condition variables + entry / exit discipline. The compiler inserts lock/unlock around every monitor method — you never call lock() by hand.

wait(cv) releases the lock and blocks on cv. signal(cv) wakes one waiter, who re-acquires the lock. That re-check is why every wait lives inside a while loop.
