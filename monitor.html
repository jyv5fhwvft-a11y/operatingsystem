Process Synchronization

Monitors

A high-level synchronization construct that wraps shared data inside a protective room — only one thread may be inside at a time, and condition variables let a thread politely step out and wait without hogging the lock.

Definition

A monitor is a synchronization construct that combines a mutex with one or more condition variables. It encapsulates shared data together with the procedures that operate on that data, and guarantees that at most one thread executes inside the monitor at any time.

Inside the monitor, wait(cv) atomically releases the lock and blocks the caller on condition variable cv; signal(cv) wakes one waiter. A thread that wakes must re-acquire the monitor lock before continuing — and in Mesa semantics it must re-check its predicate in a while loop, because the condition may no longer hold.

Two Analogies

🎒 5th Grader Analogy

Imagine a bathroom with one key. Only the kid holding the key can go inside — that's the monitor. Everyone else waits in line outside.

If you get inside and discover the toilet paper is out, you can't just sit there hogging the key forever. You step out to the waiting bench (that's wait()) and hand the key to the next kid. When the janitor restocks (that's signal()), you get back in line.

Rule: one kid inside at a time. Waiting kids always give up the key.

⚙️ Technical Analogy

A monitor = implicit mutex + condition variables + entry / exit discipline. The compiler inserts lock/unlock around every monitor method, so you never call lock() by hand.

Threads queue at the entry. Inside, wait(cv) releases the lock and blocks on cv. signal(cv) wakes one waiter — who then must re-acquire the lock. That re-check is why every wait lives in a while loop.
